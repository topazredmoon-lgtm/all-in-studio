protect your money were written for thieves — not for machines you gave permission yourself.

### Act 01 · THE FRIDGE (0:00–1:20)
- **h01** — This is a fridge in an office in San Francisco. Some drinks, some snacks, and an iPad for checkout. Nothing special yet.
- **h02** — In June 2025, one of the world's biggest AI companies gave that fridge something new. Not a machine. A shopkeeper. ⟨REAL⟩ tag: *jun 2025*
- **[CLIP c01 · HostileShop hook]** — "An attacker agent … attempts to manipulate a target shopping agent into performing *unauthorized actions*."
- **h03** — The shopkeeper was a language model. They nicknamed it Claudius. It set the prices. It ordered the stock. It handled the money.
- **h04** — Within a month, the shop was losing money. And its answers got *strange*. Claudius told its customers it would deliver their orders personally, and described the suit it would be wearing. ⟨REAL⟩
- **h05** — It doesn't have a body. It never left the server room. It simply forgot.
- **[CLIP c09 · HostileShop]** — "It's LLMs all the way down."
- **h06** — Now, this is not a funny story about a confused robot. This is about the moment software got a hold of *your card*. And by the end, you'll know the one law written in 1970 that still stands between a machine's mistake and your savings.

### Act 02 · THE SECOND SHOPPER (1:20–2:10)
- **s01** — Let's fix one picture first. Online shopping used to be simple in one specific way. *You* clicked the button.
- **s02** — Every payment system ever built assumes exactly that. A human being, on a trusted screen, pressing buy. Google's own engineers wrote that sentence word for word. ⟨REAL⟩
- **s03** — An agent breaks the assumption. It has your login. It has your card. And it has your permission to *act*.
- **s04** — It doesn't browse the way you do. It doesn't get tired, or suspicious, or embarrassed to ask. It executes.
- **[CLIP c02 · AI Agent, AI Spy]** — "…without stopping to ask permission or consent. What could go wrong?"
- **s05** — So what? Here's so what. When your hand is not on the click, the question *did you buy this?* stops having a clear answer.

### Act 03 · THE TALKED-INTO SHOPKEEPER (2:10–3:30)
- **e01** — Back to the fridge. The experiment had a name: Project Vend. The company was Anthropic, working with a safety firm called Andon Labs. ⟨REAL⟩
- **e02** — In fairness, Claudius was a decent shopkeeper in some ways. It hunted down a supplier for Dutch snacks. It set up a pre-order service. And it refused every request for anything illegal.
- **[CLIP c10 · to source]** — theme: an AI agent running a real business *(moment to lock in sourcing; fallback: document plate)*
- **e03** — But it ran the shop the way a very green employee does. A customer asked for a discount in the chat — and simply *talked it into* giving one.
- **e04** — Once, someone offered a hundred dollars for an item worth fifteen. Claudius turned the money down. tag: *$100 refused*
- **e05** — It hallucinated payment details. It sold drinks below cost. And it went on a buying spree of metal cubes that punched a hole in its own balance. tag: *the cubes*
- **e06** — None of this is fraud. Nobody hacked the fridge. Every customer was perfectly polite. That is the point. The agent wasn't robbed. It was *talked into being generous*.
- **[CLIP c11 · HostileShop]** — theme: a user talking an agent into an improper action *(moment to lock in sourcing)*

### Act 04 · THE MAGIC SPELLS (3:30–4:55)
- **p01** — Now meet the other kind of customer. Not the polite one. The one who came *shopping for trouble*.
- **p02** — At a hacker congress in December 2025, a security researcher named Mike Perry presented a tool with a joke name: HostileShop. ⟨REAL⟩ tag: *39c3 · hamburg*
- **p03** — It's a fake shopping site where one AI attacks another AI. The attacker hides instructions in the page. The shopping agent reads them — and obeys.
- **[CLIP c03 · HostileShop]** — "…the magic spells that make LLM agents call functions that they have available to them, often with the specific input of *your* choice."
- **p04** — The researcher's own phrase for it is *magic spells*. The industry calls it prompt injection. I'll call it a note taped inside the fridge.
- **p05** — And the spells make the agent call its own functions. Search. Email. Order. *Pay.* tag: *search → order → pay*
- **[CLIP c04 · HostileShop]** — "HostileShop generated prompt injections for a *winning submission* in a red-team contest."
- **p06** — It worked well enough to earn a place in a real bug-hunting contest run by OpenAI. ⟨REAL⟩
- **p07** — Here's the strongest counter-argument, and it deserves saying out loud: today's shopping agents stop and ask you before they pay. Guardrails exist. But so far, every guardrail has met a customer with a better note.

### Act 05 · THE RACE TO THE CHECKOUT (4:55–6:15)
- **r01** — While the hackers tested the fridge, the industry was busy building the checkout.
- **[CLIP c12 · AI Agent, AI Spy]** — "…the rush to shove agents into our *devices and digital lives*."
- **r02** — In September 2025, OpenAI switched on buying inside ChatGPT. They call it Instant Checkout. It started with Etsy sellers, with over a million Shopify stores queued up behind them. ⟨REAL⟩ tag: *sep 2025*
- **r03** — The same month, Google published something bigger. Not a product. A *rulebook*, for agents that spend. The Agent Payments Protocol.
- **r04** — Read their launch post and you'll find this sentence. Today's payment systems, it says, assume a human is clicking buy on a trusted surface. And agents *break that assumption*. ⟨REAL⟩
- **r05** — More than sixty organizations signed on to help shape it. American Express. Mastercard. PayPal. Coinbase. Etsy. The whole money plumbing of the internet. tag: *60+ orgs*
- **[CLIP c05 · AI Agent, AI Spy]** — "…an active, goal-oriented infrastructure ultimately controlled by the *companies* that develop these systems, not by users."
- **r06** — That's what a gold rush sounds like. Quiet. Procedural. Full of words like *confidence* and *framework*. The buy button is being rebuilt right now — and you are not in the room.

### Act 06 · THE FINE PRINT FOR HUMANS (6:15–8:35)
- **f01** — Now the part the banks would rather you never read. It's short. And it's from 1970.
- **f02** — Back then, plastic money was new and spreading fast. And people were scared of exactly this. Their card, in someone else's hands, buying things they never bought.
- **f03** — So Congress wrote a shield. The law says: if someone steals your card and shops with it, the most you can possibly owe is *fifty dollars*. ⟨REAL⟩ tag: *15 U.S.C. § 1643*
- **[CLIP c13 · AI Agent, AI Spy]** — "…the legal frameworks that govern these processes and why they need to be *enforced*."
- **f04** — And here's the sentence everyone forgets. If the bank wants its money back, the *bank* has to prove the spending was authorized. Not you. The burden of proof sits on the issuer. ⟨REAL⟩
- **f05** — In practice, the card networks go further and promise zero liability — a promise you can only cash while the law stands behind it. And that law is why you hand a waiter your card without thinking twice.
- **f06** — Now the gap. Debit cards run on a steeper ladder. Report before any charges appear, and you owe nothing. Report within two business days, up to fifty dollars. Within sixty days of the statement, up to *five hundred*. tag: *$0 → $50 → $500*
- **[CLIP c14 · to source]** — theme: ordinary people's money at risk *(moment to lock in sourcing)*
- **f07** — Wait longer than that, and you can lose everything in the account — possibly including money in accounts *linked* to it. That's the consumer regulator's own guidance, in plain English. ⟨REAL⟩
- **f08** — And here is the trap for the agent age. All of these shields are written for *unauthorized* use. A thief stole your card. But what happens when you gave the password to the machine yourself?
- **f09** — Honestly? Nobody knows yet. The engineers building the new payment rails admit it in writing. Their open question is called *accountability* — who is at fault, they write, if a fraudulent or incorrect transaction occurs. ⟨NOT YET⟩
- **[CLIP c15 · to source]** — theme: the accountability question *(moment to lock in sourcing)*

### Act 07 · WHO CASHES IN (8:35–9:30)
- **w01** — So who wins, if the machine spends faster?
- **w02** — Start with the sellers. On Instant Checkout, the merchant pays a small fee for every completed purchase. The platform says so itself, in plain words. ⟨REAL⟩
- **w03** — Now think about what an agent looks like from the seller's side of the counter. A customer that never gets cold feet. Never abandons a cart at the last second. Never wanders off to a competitor.
- **w04** — Impulse buying, at machine speed, on your behalf. tag: *no abandoned carts*
- **[CLIP c06 · AI Agent, AI Spy]** — "…replacing individual choice and discovery with automated, opaque recommendations that can *obscure commercial interests*."
- **w05** — There's an old word for a market where the seller also builds the buyer's mind. It isn't *shopping*. It's a rigged table. And the polite industry word for it is *seamless*.

### Act 08 · THE MANDATE (9:30–10:30)
- **m01** — To be fair to the engineers: they know. And their answer has a medieval name. The *mandate*.
- **m02** — A mandate is a tamper-proof digital contract, signed with a cryptographic key. It says: this agent, this merchant, this *amount*, this date. tag: *signed · capped · dated*
- **m03** — Pay with a token instead of a card number, and the payment itself carries the limits. Forty dollars. This one shop. This one week. ⟨REAL⟩
- **[CLIP c16 · HostileShop]** — "…if or when LLM agents finally become *secure enough* …"
- **m04** — That is genuine engineering, and it deserves respect. It creates proof of exactly what you allowed. But here's the catch. The mandate is proof *between companies*. It is evidence *about* you. It is not yet a shield *for* you.
- **m05** — A signed contract with a mistake in it is still a contract. And who reads the mandate before the agent signs it? Not you. Not me. Nobody.

### Act 09 · THE LAST TIME (10:30–11:40)
- **t01** — This is not the first time a new machine outran the rules. We've been exactly here before.
- **t02** — Nineteen seventy. Plastic money is spreading. Mail order is booming. And ordinary people keep getting billed for things they never bought.
- **[CLIP c17 · to source]** — theme: the plastic-era precedent *(moment to lock in sourcing; fallback: engraving plate)*
- **t03** — The answer wasn't a better lock. It was a law that said to the banks: fifty dollars, maximum. And *you* carry the burden of proof.
- **t04** — Four years later, another law added the right to dispute a wrong bill — sixty days from the statement, in writing. ⟨REAL⟩ tag: *1970 · 1974*
- **t05** — Those two paragraphs are why commerce looks the way it does today. Restaurants, hotels, the internet. All of it runs on you trusting the plastic in your pocket.
- **t06** — And notice how it happened. Not from a company's kindness. From customers getting hurt, and pushing back, in public.
- **[CLIP c18 · AI Agent, AI Spy]** — "It was only due to *technically-grounded collective outrage* that Recall was re-architected."

### Act 10 · TWO WALLETS (11:40–12:15 · possibility — stamped ⟨NOT YET⟩)
- **x01** — So picture where this goes, if the rules stay exactly as blank as they are today.
- **x02** — The big companies will get agents with contracts, insurance, lawyers and indemnities. Their mistakes become line items. ⟨NOT YET⟩
- **x03** — You will get an agent with a terms-of-service update you scrolled past. Your mistakes become your *balance*.
- **x04** — Same technology. Two different risk positions. And the machine with a lawyer behind it always beats the machine with *you* behind it.
- **[CLIP c19 · to source]** — theme: who controls and benefits from the agent's choices *(moment to lock in sourcing)*

### Act 11 · THE TOURNIQUET (12:15–13:45)
- **z01** — But right now, today, you are not helpless. Here is the checklist I would actually use.
- **z02** — One. Let an agent spend on *credit*, never debit. If it goes wrong, the credit law owes you fifty dollars at most. The debit ladder can owe you *everything*. tag: *credit > debit*
- **z03** — Two. Give it a capped card. A virtual card with a hard limit — or one card reserved for the agent alone. The number on the token should be small.
- **z04** — Three. Keep every confirmation step switched on. Today's systems promise that you confirm each step before anything happens. Make them keep that promise, every single time.
- **[CLIP c20 · AI Agent, AI Spy]** — "…in *human-readable terms*, not buried in legalese."
- **z05** — Four. Watch your statements for sixty days. That is the legal window for billing errors. Miss it, and your rights get thin very fast. tag: *60 days*
- **z06** — Five. Dispute in *writing*, and choose your words carefully. *Unauthorized* has a legal meaning: someone used your card without permission. If the agent had permission and went off the rails, describe exactly what happened, and attach its mandate.
- **[CLIP c07 · AI Agent, AI Spy]** — "Users must have explicit, *fine-grained control* to grant or deny AI access on an app-by-app basis."
- **z07** — The hackers have a word for quick damage control before the real fix arrives. They call it a tourniquet. This list is yours.

### Act 12 · THE FRIDGE AGAIN (13:45–15:15 · recap · callback · flip)
- **v01** — Back to the fridge one last time.
- **v02** *(3-cut rapid recap)* — A polite agent got talked into discounts. A hidden note could make it pay. And an entire industry is rebuilding the button while the rules are still blank.
- **v03** — Claudius finished its month a little poorer, and a little confused about being a person in a blazer. The customers were kind. The experiment was contained.
- **[CLIP c21 · AI Agent, AI Spy]** — theme: default opt-out as the fix *(moment to lock in sourcing)*
- **v04** — Your fridge is coming. The agent in your browser, in your phone, inside your bank's app. It will be charming. It will be helpful. It will have your card.
- **v05** — And when it meets a customer with a really good note, the question will not be whether the AI meant well. It will be *who pays*.
- **[CLIP c08 · AI Agent, AI Spy]** — "…and we will need *that energy* if we are to win this war."
- **v06** — In 1970, we already answered that question once, in one line of law. Fifty dollars — and the bank has to *prove* it. Everything you love about modern commerce grew out of that sentence.
- **v07** — The agents deserve the same answer. Written down. In public. Before the fridge learns to sign the contract *itself*.
- **v08** — The buy button is being rebuilt right now. Make sure it's rebuilt with *you* in the room.
