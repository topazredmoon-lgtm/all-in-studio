## What changes and why
1. **True beats loud.** The owner's script states predictions as facts ("within 36 months... outlawed", "a classified
   warning", "chips with kill-switches", "every prompt tied to your ID"). On a monetised channel that is a strike
   risk, and viewers who check it stop trusting us. The real story is scarier: *every piece needed for a ban already
   exists or has been tried.* So the spine becomes: **"Nobody has banned AI. But look at the pieces on the table."**
   Facts are said as facts (sourced in `SOURCES.md`); predictions are said as "if".
2. **Takeaways.** Each act lands one thing the viewer didn't know: encryption was legally a weapon until 1996; the
   US already put AI "brains" under export control once (Jan 2025); a few hundred dollars removes an open model's
   safety training; the man who runs ChatGPT asked the Senate for an AI licence; and how ordinary people won the last
   time (a book, a court case: "code is speech").
3. **Friendly jargon (owner flag ~3:47 = the old "black-market proof" scene).** Every hard word gets one plain
   sentence and a picture: *weights* (the AI's brain, a file of numbers you can download), *open vs closed*,
   *fine-tuning* (a short extra lesson), *jailbreak*, *export control*, *licence regime*, *regulatory capture*.
4. **Narrator has a side.** Neutral until the facts are laid out (act 5), then: "I'll be honest: this is the part I
   hate." Ends with hope and a job for the viewer, not doom.
5. **Ending flip.** The owner's "it will be called sedition" becomes the encryption callback: they called strong locks
   weapons once; now they're on every phone, because people fought. Same fight, and now you know the pieces.
